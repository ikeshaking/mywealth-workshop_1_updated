1. Executive summary

The SOA generation toolchain converts an adviser's structured Advice Request, plus the client's supporting documents, into a draft Statement of Advice in the firm's house style. It exists to make SOA production faster, more consistent and more complete — every recommendation the firm makes routinely arrives with its approved recommendation wording, "why this benefits you" reasoning, risks, and alternatives considered, drawn from a single reviewed word bank rather than re-typed (and re-varied) for each file.

Three design principles run through the whole system:

  • One source of truth for wording. All tailored wording lives in the Words Masterlist (164 modules). The Advice Request Builder's strategy ticks map 1:1 onto it — verified programmatically, currently zero unmapped ticks — so nothing an adviser can request will silently arrive without approved wording.
  • Fail loudly, never silently. Anything the system cannot confidently resolve is either surfaced in a post-import "still needs your attention" report, or left in the document as an unmissable placeholder ($XXX, yellow-highlighted bracket choices). The system never guesses between two plausible matches.
  • Human in the loop. The output is a draft. The adviser/paraplanner confirms every file mapping before import, resolves every flagged gap, and reviews the document before it goes near a client. The tool standardises wording and assembly; it does not determine whether advice is appropriate.
This revision (v192). The toolchain is now ready for retirement / pension scenarios (Transition to Retirement and Account-Based Pension). Two independent pieces of work, made against the same base, were reconciled into one build: a retirement-projection engine (pension income & balance auto-fill, the income-drawdown "Reaching Your Targets" rows, and the "how long your money will last" longevity narrative) and a TTR/ABP advice-request engine (tolerant word-bank resolution for the merged super area, and auto scope-in plus an auto-added TTR goal for an existing client on a retirement SOA). The word bank grew from 163 to 164 modules (a new area-0 TTR standing-rule goal) and the Account-Based Pension "draw an income" module was enriched with its outcome, why and risks. See Section 9 and Section 11.

3. The three roles & system components

The single most important thing to understand about the toolchain is that it has three parts that do three different jobs. Confusing them is what causes drift and mistakes.

How they interact, in one sentence: the Advice Request says "commence a TTR pension" → the Generator looks that strategy up in the Word Bank → pulls the approved TTR recommendation / why / risks / alternatives → writes them into the right section of the SOA, filling any dollar figures from the imported data.

What lives where — the rule that prevents drift:

  • Wording (what a recommendation says) lives only in the Masterlist JSON. Edit it there, mirror the docx, then drop the JSON onto the generator. Never hand-edit the generator's copy of the word bank — that is exactly what caused an earlier JSON/generator drift.
  • Behaviour (scenario switches, figure computation, table layout, safety nets — e.g. accumulation-vs-retirement, the pension income & "how long" logic) lives in the generator's engine code. It is not wording and cannot be changed through the masterlist; it is a developer change, gated by the regression suites.
  • Client facts & choices (which strategies, scope, products, fees, figures) live in the per-client Advice Request and the supporting documents — never baked into the tools.

3.1 Advice Request Builder (Advice_Request_Builder.html)

A single-file web form the adviser completes after the strategy meeting. It captures: SOA type (accumulation / retirement), client(s) and adviser, new / existing status, entities (SMSF / company / trust), the "Hi team" narrative, risk profile per client (and per entity), scope in / scope out per advice area, the strategy ticks (across the advice areas, each with the firm's default recommendation line and any dollar/percentage slots), product selections (super platform, recommended insurer per client), and the fee matrix. It exports the Advice Request as JSON (machine-readable, the generator's source of truth) and as a Word document (the human-readable paraplanning request). Each tick is linked to a word-bank module by Area :: name; free-text in the narrative is matched by the module's triggerPhrases as a fallback.

3.2 Words Masterlist (SOA_Words_MasterList.json / .docx)

The firm's tailored-wording register: 164 modules, of which 148 are linked to builder ticks and 16 are standing rules (openers, scope wording, CARE philosophy justifications, and — new in v192 — the retirement TTR review-meeting goal). Each module holds the trigger (tick name), keyword trigger phrases, mode and scope gate, and the SOA wording: recommendation line, why, risks, alternatives, outcome. The Word version is generated from the JSON, so the two cannot drift. Section 5 covers how it is matched and edited.

3.3 SOA Generator (SOA_Generator_v192.html)

A single-file web application (runs locally in a browser; no server, no data leaves the machine). It holds the SOA as 36 sections ("blocks"), each with standard wording plus the tailored content written in by imports. Its smart drop accepts any batch of files, classifies each, routes it to the right client, and applies everything in a fixed, safe order. A live preview mirrors the Word output; export produces the .docx via the embedded docx library. v192 is a clean merge of two engine change-sets (see Section 11); it carries the 164-module word bank re-synced from the masterlist.

4. End-to-end data flow

  1. Adviser completes the Advice Request Builder — ticks strategies, sets scope, risk profiles, products, fees and entities; downloads the Advice Request JSON (+ Word copy).
  2. Paraplanner gathers the supporting documents — Wealth Projector fact-find JSON, data collection sheets, CARE portfolio doc, WealthSolver comparison, Risk Researcher, insurer quotes, existing-cover quote, commission reports.
  3. Everything is dropped onto the generator in one batch — each file is read, classified, and client/partner routing is proposed; the paraplanner confirms the mapping in the smart-drop modal before anything is applied.
  4. Deterministic apply pipeline — regardless of drop order: word bank → fact-find JSON → Advice Request → data collection → CARE → WealthSolver → Risk Researcher → insurer quotes → existing-cover quote → commissions. Identity loads before per-person documents; premiums exist before commission maths.
  5. Advice Request drives the word bank — each strategy tick is looked up (exact key → normalised → unique containment; never a guess between two candidates). Matched modules write the recommendation / why / risks / alternatives into the right SOA section; scope flags activate sections; framing items set new/existing-client wording; entities activate the entity section; retirement triggers set the SOA type and (for TTR/ABP) auto scope-in and add the retirement goal. Every miss is recorded and reported.
  6. Gap & conflict reporting, then human editing — the import summary lists what applied, what was skipped, importer warnings, unmatched strategies and empty key figures. A conflicts panel flags scope-vs-projector mismatches. Unresolved figures remain as $XXX / yellow placeholders that cannot be missed.
  7. Render safety nets, then Word export — on every render and again at export: scaffolding stripped, template conditionals resolved for the scenario, per-person tables built for couples, duplicates de-duplicated, product defaults applied, cost lines computed, token backfills. The exported .docx matches the live preview.

5. The word-bank concept

Every module is keyed "Area::Strategy name" (e.g. "5 · Personal Superannuation::Commence a pension / transition to retirement (TTR)") and carries five wording fields:

FieldLands in the SOA as
strat (strategiesLine)The recommendation bullet(s) in the strategy table — dollar slots filled from the builder's amounts, per-person expansion for couples where the strategy is per-person
why"Why this benefits you" list — de-duplicated across modules landing in the same section
risksThe section's risk list — de-duplicated
alts (alternatives)"Alternatives considered" — including why each was discounted
outcome (outcomesLine)Summary — Outcomes line where applicable

Matching is tolerant but never speculative. A tick is matched to a module by exact key first; failing that, by a normalised form that ignores case, separators, punctuation and area numbering; failing that, by containment — but only if exactly one candidate contains (or is contained by) the tick name. Two plausible candidates = no match = a recorded miss, reported to the paraplanner. A missed tick still lands in the SOA as its builder default text, so nothing the adviser requested disappears — it just arrives without the why/risks/alternatives, and flagged. New in v192: the resolver also tolerates a merged builder area ("Personal Superannuation & Contributions") by resolving it to the word bank's separate "Personal Superannuation" and "Superannuation Contributions" areas, routing each strategy to its true block.

Coverage is complete and provable. A three-way reconciliation (builder ticks ↔ word bank ↔ masterlist) is part of the release checks: all builder ticks currently resolve, and the bank and masterlist are identical at 164 modules in both directions.

The wording is editable without touching the tools. Dropping an edited SOA_Words_MasterList.json onto the generator replaces its live word bank (adds, rewordings and removals take effect, with counts reported). Dropping a small fragment keyed "Area::Name" merges — for adding or rewording individual modules. The masterlist Word document is generated from the JSON, so the readable register always matches what the tools actually use.

This is the compliance heart of the system: the wording clients receive is not whatever a paraplanner typed that day — it is the reviewed register, applied mechanically, with every exception flagged.

The firm's existing verification (carried forward, unchanged, as the regression gate):

  • Automated regression: 12 headless-browser test suites (~180 individual checks) covering import classification and routing, couple rendering across all four table families, CARE workflows, insurer comparison maths, drop-order independence, the ATP compliance block, word-bank matching and editability, entity consumption, and the render safety nets — run against the actual HTML file.
  • Exemplar parity: output verified against six real SOAs spanning the scenario matrix, plus the Borgh SOA (52 conformance checks) and the Robinson insurance section (13/13), re-verified on the exported Word document (Rounds 15–16), and Fisher (OneCare split + AustralianSuper existing-increase).
  • Reviewer-comment cycle: 90+ annotated comments across Robinson, Borgh, Fisher triaged, fixed and re-verified.
  • Coverage reconciliation: builder ticks ↔ word bank ↔ masterlist checked programmatically in both directions.

Verification added for this revision (v192), 8 July 2026 — headless Chromium against the shipped SOA_Generator_v192.html:

  • Merge integrity: the two engine change-sets occupy disjoint line ranges (state/rendering vs the v200 advice-request engine); the 3-way merge produced zero conflicts and both feature sets are present and callable.
  • Retirement path: pension figures compute from inputs (worked example: starting balance $247,628 → income $45,000 p.a. / $3,750 p.m.); the Reaching-Your-Targets income rows and the Projected-Outcomes longevity tail render in the preview; the accumulation path is a clean regression (no income rows, no longevity tail).
  • TTR/ABP advice-request path: a retirement advice request with a TTR strategy auto-selects Retirement, auto scopes-in the TTR line, and auto-adds the TTR review-meeting goal.

10. Known limitations & human checkpoints

  • The tool assembles and standardises; it does not assess suitability. The Advice Request remains the adviser's professional determination.
  • Figures are only as good as the source documents. A wrong balance in the fact-find flows through — the gap report catches missing figures, not incorrect ones. The pension income auto-fill is a starting estimate (an explicit income-draw goal figure wins; otherwise a max-10% TTR estimate of the balance) and must be checked against the adviser's intended drawdown.
  • The "how long your money will last" content is a narrative plus the adviser's attached How Long Projector spreadsheet — the generator does not itself simulate drawdown-to-depletion.
  • Free-text strategies outside the ticks arrive without why/risks/alternatives and are flagged. If a custom strategy becomes routine, add it to the masterlist (Section 11).
  • Scanned/image-only PDFs cannot be read; those figures are keyed manually and shown in the gap report. Annexures are produced separately.
Mandatory checkpoints before an SOA leaves paraplanning: (1) the smart-drop completion report shows no unresolved skips or warnings; (2) the attention list is empty; (3) no $XXX or yellow-bracket placeholder remains — including the pension income figures on a retirement SOA; (4) the conflicts panel is clear; (5) full read-through of the draft. These are procedural — the tool surfaces them but people close them.

11. Change management & the v192 merge

  • Wording changes are made in SOA_Words_MasterList.json (the source of truth), mirrored to the Word register, reviewed like any advice-wording change, then dropped onto the generator — which reports exactly how many modules were added, updated and removed. No code changes, no redeployment. Direction is always JSON → generator; never hand-edit the generator's word bank.
  • New builder ticks require a matching masterlist module (same area and name, builderTick:true, identical trigger). The three-way reconciliation check is the release gate: zero unmatched ticks before a builder version ships.
  • Behaviour changes (scenario logic, figure computation, table layout — e.g. the retirement projection engine) are developer changes to the generator, gated by the regression suites. They do not go through the masterlist.
  • Tool versions are single files with version-stamped names; superseded versions are retained.
The v192 merge (8 July 2026). Two edit streams were made independently against the same v190_38r base: a retirement-projection engine (state + rendering) and a TTR/ABP advice-request engine (the v200 engine). Because they touch disjoint code, they were reconciled by a clean 3-way merge (no conflicts) into SOA_Generator_v192.html. In parallel, the two masterlist edits — the new area-0 TTR goal and the enriched ABP module — were combined into a single 164-module JSON, the docx was regenerated from it, and the generator's word bank was re-synced from that JSON (1 added / 163 updated / 0 removed). Use v192; discard the interim split builds. This episode is also the lesson behind the sync rule above: the earlier drift happened because a word-bank change was made in the generator and never exported back to the JSON.

This SOP takes you from a finished strategy meeting to a draft SOA in Word. Click a step header to open or close it; tick the checklists as you work (saved in this browser). Nothing is applied to the SOA without your confirmation, and anything the tools can't resolve is flagged for you — never guessed.

Who this is for. The associate producing the SOA. The adviser makes the advice decisions at the strategy meeting and signs off the final document; the associate operates the toolchain and prepares the draft.

The three parts — Advice Request vs Word Bank vs Generator

▾

Before anything else, get these three straight. They do three different jobs, and mixing them up is what causes mistakes and "drift".

Advice Request
(from the Builder)		Words Masterlist

Answers"Which strategies, scope, products, fees — for whom?""How do we word this strategy, and its why / risks / alternatives?""Put it together in house style for this scenario"

Think of it asThe orderThe recipe bookThe kitchen
How they work together: the Advice Request says "commence a TTR pension" → the Generator looks that up in the Word Bank → pulls the approved TTR recommendation, why, risks and alternatives → writes them into the SOA and fills in the dollar figures from the imported documents.
The golden rule that prevents drift. Wording changes go in the Masterlist JSON (then mirror the docx, then drop the JSON onto the generator). Never retype wording directly inside the generator — if you do, the JSON falls behind and the two disagree. Behaviour (how the document is built, the retirement projections, table layouts) is code in the generator and is not something you change through wording at all.

Complete the Advice Request Builder

▾

Open Advice_Request_Builder.html in your browser. The builder is the source of truth for what this SOA recommends — everything you tick here arrives in the SOA with the firm's approved wording attached.

Save your work regularly. Use 💾 Save to download a working-state file you can reopen with ⬆ Load. The file the generator actually imports is the ⬇ .json export (advice_request.json). ⬇ Download Word is the human-readable copy for the client file. Always confirm your strategy ticks are present before exporting — an advice request with no ticks produces an SOA with derived super/insurance wording only, and the generator flags it loudly.

Set the SOA type first

Choose Wealth accumulation or Retirement at the top. This one choice drives the goal set, the summaries, and the projected-outcomes section (see the Retirement section below). Then set client(s), new/existing status, entities, risk profiles, scope in/out, the strategy ticks (with any dollar/percentage amounts), products, and the fee matrix.

Open the generator and set the SOA type

▾

Open SOA_Generator_v192.html. It runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded. At the top, pick the same SOA type you chose in the builder (accumulation / retirement). If you import a retirement advice request, the generator sets this for you.

What "Retirement" changes: the generic goals swap to retirement/income goals; the "How Long Projector" annexure is enabled; and the projected-outcomes section will show the Pathway-to-Wealth table plus the "how long your money will last" longevity narrative. (Details in the Retirement section.)

4

Smart drop — import everything in one batch

▾

Drag the whole batch (advice request JSON + all supporting documents) onto the generator at once. It reads and classifies each file, proposes the client/partner routing, and shows you the smart-drop modal.

Confirm the mapping before you apply. Check every file's detected type and the person it's routed to. Anything it isn't sure about is set to "skip" or asks you explicitly — fix it here. Order doesn't matter: the generator always applies files in a fixed, safe order regardless of how you dropped them.

5

Resolve gaps and conflicts

▾

After import, work the attention list and the conflicts panel to zero. These are the tool doing its job — surfacing everything it could not confidently resolve.

  • Unmatched strategies — a ticked strategy that didn't match a word-bank module lands as its builder default text, without why/risks. Either it's a free-text/custom strategy (complete it manually) or the tick/module names disagree (fix the masterlist — see Wording updates).
  • Empty key figures — anything showing $XXX or a yellow bracket. On a retirement SOA this includes the pension income figures if no income-draw amount or starting balance was available; enter the adviser's intended drawdown.
  • Conflicts — e.g. scope vs projector mismatches; resolve or acknowledge each.
Checklist
6

Review and edit section by section

▾

Read the draft top to bottom in the live preview. Edit any section directly. The preview is exactly what exports, so what you fix here is what the client gets.

Remember the golden rule. If you find wording that's wrong for every SOA (not just this client), don't just fix it here — fix it in the Masterlist so it's right next time (see Wording updates). Fixing it only in this document leaves the firm library wrong.

Export to Word and finalise

▾

Export the .docx. The export runs the same safety-net pipeline as the preview, so the Word document matches what you reviewed. Attach the annexures (produced separately), including the How Long Projector snapshot for a retirement SOA.

Retirement / pension SOAs (TTR & ABP) v192

▾

A retirement SOA is produced exactly the same way — you just choose Retirement as the SOA type and tick the retirement strategies in the builder. Here is what changes, and where each piece comes from.

What you do

  1. In the builder, set SOA type = Retirement, and tick the relevant strategy under Personal Superannuation: Commence a pension / transition to retirement (TTR) or Commence an Account-Based Pension and draw an income (and recontribution / seamless transfer / reversionary as needed). Fill the pension balance / income amount on the tick.
  2. Import as normal. The generator auto-selects Retirement, scopes-in the pension line, and — for an existing client — auto-adds the TTR review-meeting goal.
  3. Check the auto-filled pension figures (see below), and attach your How Long Projector snapshot to the annexure.

What the SOA shows

SectionOn a retirement SOA
GoalsRetirement/income goals (budgeting goal removed); existing-client TTR goal auto-added
Reaching Your TargetsThe usual Pathway-to-Wealth table plus income rows: Existing Benefits to Draw Income From, Desired Pension Withdraw income (p.m.), and an Average Return assumption
Projected OutcomesThe Pathway-to-Wealth table plus the "how long your money will last" narrative — "…withdraw $X p.a. tax-free in perpetuity…" with the figures filled in
How Long Projector annexureEnabled — attach your own projector spreadsheet snapshot (the generator does not simulate drawdown itself)
Check the pension figures. The income (p.a./p.m.) and starting balance auto-fill from the imported projection and the retirement income-draw goal. If no income amount was supplied, the generator uses a max-10% TTR estimate of the balance as a starting point — always confirm it matches the adviser's intended drawdown. If nothing is known it stays $XXX for you to complete.
TTR vs Account-Based Pension. TTR is for a client still working (max 10% drawdown; supplement income). An ABP is for a retired client / one who has met a condition of release (minimum drawdown; income "in perpetuity"). The Summary Strategies, Products and Outcomes read whichever pension strategy you ticked and render the matching wording from the word bank — so a TTR SOA reads TTR (not ABP), and the body and summary agree.
Set the SOA type in the generator, and it wins. The retirement-vs-accumulation choice lives in the generator's inputs tab, not the advice request. Importing an advice request will not flip a type you've already chosen. Either order works — set the type then import, or import then set the type — the TTR strategies, products, outcomes and the TTR review-meeting goal come through the same way.
W

Updating the wording (the word bank)

▾

When the firm wants to change what a recommendation says (anywhere it appears), you change the Masterlist — never the generator directly.

The procedure

  1. Edit the wording in SOA_Words_MasterList.json — find the module by its Area :: name and change its strat / why / risks / alternatives / outcome.
  2. Mirror the change into the .docx so the readable register matches the JSON. (The docx is generated from the JSON — keep them in step.)
  3. Have the change reviewed like any advice-wording change.
  4. Drop the JSON onto the generator. A full masterlist replaces the whole word bank (so deletions and renames take effect); a small fragment keyed "Area::Name" merges (handy for one module). The generator reports "N added / N updated / N removed" — check those counts are what you expect (a re-sync of the full list should report 0 removed).

Adding a new strategy

A new builder tick needs a matching masterlist module with the same Area :: name and builderTick:true; keep the trigger identical and add triggerPhrases for free-text matching. The release check reconciles builder ticks ↔ word bank ↔ masterlist — there must be zero unmatched ticks.

Direction is always JSON → generator. The one time this went wrong, someone edited the generator's word bank directly and never exported it back to the JSON, so the JSON and docx fell behind. If that ever happens, reconcile by pulling the generator's live bank back into the masterlist, then resume editing the JSON as the source of truth.

Troubleshooting

▾
SymptomWhat to do
An SOA came out with super/insurance wording only and an "attention" warningThe advice request was exported with no strategy ticks (often the builder page was reopened without loading the saved state). Re-open the builder, Load your state (or re-tick), re-export the JSON, re-import.
A ticked strategy has no why/risks/alternativesIt didn't match a word-bank module. If it's a real firm strategy, the tick name and module Area :: name disagree — fix the masterlist and drop it. If it's a one-off custom strategy, complete the wording manually.
Pension income shows $XXX on a retirement SOANo income-draw amount / starting balance was available. Enter the adviser's intended drawdown, or supply the projection/income-draw goal and re-import.
Wording I changed in the generator isn't there next timeExpected — the generator is not where wording lives. Make the change in the Masterlist JSON, mirror the docx, and drop the JSON (see Wording updates).
A dropped masterlist reported unexpected "removed" modulesYou dropped a partial or older full masterlist (a full masterlist replaces everything). Re-drop the current, complete JSON.
The Word export looks different from the previewThey run the same pipeline, so this should not happen — re-run the firm's docx-export regression check and report it; do not ship.
